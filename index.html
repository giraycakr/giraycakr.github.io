About

I am a final-year computer engineering student at Istanbul Medeniyet University, applying to PhD programs for Fall 2027. I am interested in understanding what neural networks actually learn, and in using that understanding to make models safer. My undergraduate research asked a simple question: when data augmentation improves accuracy, what changes inside the model? Answering it taught me how easily a single explanation metric can mislead, which is what draws me to interpretability and LLM safety.

Research

Manuscript under review (2026). Sep 2025 – Jul 2026.

Do data augmentation methods improve accuracy through the same mechanism?

Question: Augmentation reliably improves image classification, but why it helps is rarely measured. I compared seven configurations (baseline, traditional, Cutout, Random Erasing, Mixup, CutMix, Mixup+CutMix) on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18, using four explainability lenses at once: multi-layer Grad-CAM, attention entropy and coverage, silhouette analysis of the 512-dimensional feature space, and occlusion sensitivity. 42 training runs (7 methods × 2 datasets × 3 seeds), all under identical hyperparameters.

Findings:

  • Methods with similar accuracy work differently inside the model. On CIFAR-10, Mixup lowers the feature-space silhouette score. CutMix leaves it nearly unchanged but narrows attention onto the object, and its decisions rely on several object regions instead of one.
  • A widely held claim did not survive a ceiling-effect check. Per-class gains from CutMix look strongly tied to class difficulty on CIFAR-100, but after normalizing by the remaining accuracy gap the relationship vanishes: CutMix closes a similar share of the gap for every class.
  • Some metrics can't separate the methods. Attention entropy is saturated in this setting, and per-class attention changes don't predict which classes gain the most.
  • Takeaway: judging augmentation, or any intervention, through a single explanation metric can be misleading. Multiple, complementary measurements are needed.

Paper, code and figures will be linked once the review process allows.

What I want to work on next

I want to bring the same multi-angle skepticism to language models: checking whether attribution-based and representation-based explanations of an LLM agree, and using interpretability to detect and understand unsafe behavior such as backdoors, instead of trusting a single signal.
